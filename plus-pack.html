How it fits together

The modules are not separate documents. They are one chain, and the platform keeps the links.

  1. InputSupplierSomeone wants to buy an AI tool, or one has appeared in a tool you already own.
  2. ModuleVendor Due DiligenceStructured supplier diligence goes out before you buy, not after.
  3. ModuleVendor Risk ScoreThe answers come back and get scored against recognised framework areas.
  4. DecisionApprove, or do notA recorded go / no-go with a named decision-maker. Rejections are evidence too.
  5. ModuleUse Case RegisterAn approved tool becomes one or more logged use cases, each with an owner and the data it touches.
  6. ModuleRisk AssessmentsScore likelihood against impact. The worst score sets the use case’s rating automatically.
  7. ModuleAI Risk RegisterAnything you cannot mitigate gets an owner, a due date and a residual score.
  8. ModuleAcceptable Use PolicyWhat the assessments concluded becomes the rule staff actually read.
  9. ModuleStaff & Sign-offEveryone acknowledges the current version. Re-publishing resets it, which is the point.
  10. OutputEvidenceA dated trail from supplier to signature. This is what an auditor is really asking for.
  11. ModuleGovernance CentreAnything overdue, unassessed or unsigned surfaces here on its own.

That chain is the difference between a document set and a management system. It is also what an auditor is really asking for when they ask to see your AI governance: not the policy, but the evidence you followed it.

Why it beats a folder of documents

Export whenever you like
