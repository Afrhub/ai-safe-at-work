How it fits together

The sections are not separate documents. They are one chain, and the platform keeps the links.

  1. Vendor Due Diligence — send the questionnaire before you buy.
  2. Vendor Risk Score — score the answers, record go / no-go.
  3. Use Case Register — the approved tool becomes a logged use case.
  4. Risk Assessment — assess it, and the use case’s risk rating updates automatically from the worst score.
  5. AI Risk Register — anything you cannot mitigate gets an owner and a due date.
  6. Dashboard — anything overdue or unassessed surfaces on its own.

That chain is the difference between a document set and a management system. It is also what an auditor is really asking for when they ask to see your AI governance: not the policy, but the evidence you followed it.

Why it beats a folder of documents

A folder cannot say “this tool has no assessment” or “this mitigation is three weeks late”. The dashboard does, every time you open it.

Sign-off is tied to the version

Acknowledgements are recorded against the policy version they were given for, so rewriting the policy asks everyone to sign again. That is the bit people get wrong on paper.

Records an auditor can follow

Each record links to the one that produced it, so you can show a decision and the evidence behind it, organised the way an auditor asks for it.

It keeps up with the rules

Content is reviewed and updated at least quarterly as the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 and related standards move.

Training is included

Every Platform subscription includes the AI Safe@Work course for your staff, and completion feeds straight into your records.

Export whenever you like

Every section prints to PDF. Your evidence is yours, and leaving does not mean losing it.
