1. Before you start

The order matters

The registers reference each other. Build them in this order and nothing ever asks you to pick a record that does not exist yet:

#SectionWhy it comes here
1Staff & Sign-offOwner pickers on every other register read from staff records. Add people first and every later dropdown is populated.
2Vendor Due DiligenceThe AI Tool Register's supplier field points at vendors.
3Vendor Risk ScoreScores the diligence answers once they come back.
4AI Tool RegisterUse cases point at tools, and Section 3 of the policy is generated from this register.
5Use Case RegisterRisks and assessments both reference use cases.
6Risk AssessmentsSets the use case's risk rating automatically.
7AI Risk RegisterOptionally links to a use case.
8Acceptable Use PolicyGenerated from the registers. Do it last or Section 3 is thin.
9Incidents, RACI, ToRIndependent, do them whenever.

Northgate Legal, the worked example

A 42-person law firm. Two partners, a practice manager, a small IT contractor. They have Copilot on business licences, several people using personal ChatGPT accounts, and a contract-review tool a partner signed up for without telling anyone. That last one is the reason the AI Tool Register exists.

One habit worth forming. The Governance Centre list is generated from your registers, never typed. An item disappears when the underlying record is fixed. So work top down, fix the record, and check back rather than trying to clear the list directly.

3. Vendor Due Diligence

Nav: Vendor Due Diligence. Step 1 of vendor onboarding. Send it before you buy.

#FieldNorthgate Legal example
1Click + Add vendor
2Vendor nameLexvia Ltd
3Product / toolLexvia Contract Review, Professional tier
4Vendor contact (name / email)Ana Bright / ana.bright@lexvia.example
5Diligence checklistTick each question as the supplier answers it. There are 23 must-answer questions.
6NotesAnswers received 14 Aug. Sub-processor list incomplete, chased.

What the 23 questions cover

Legal entity and group structure; the tool and tier; underlying foundation models; countries of processing and storage; an EU-only processing option; retention of prompts, responses, metadata and embeddings; a contractual commitment that your data is not used for training; deletion on request including backups and sub-processors; the full sub-processor list and change notification; a DPA with SCCs; EU AI Act Article 26 deployer-facing instructions; a current ISO 27001 certificate and Statement of Applicability; SSO, SCIM, MFA and RBAC; per-model provider, version and limitations; whether any model is high-risk under Annex III; cross-tenant isolation controls; a contractual 72-hour breach notification SLA; the Article 73 serious-incident process; advance notice of material changes; and four Cyber Resilience Act questions covering scope, the Article 14 disclosure contact, declaration of conformity and CE marking, and an SBOM with the declared security-update support period.

Why ask a SaaS vendor about the CRA. Pure software as a service is usually outside CRA scope. The answer still tells you which kind of supplier you are dealing with, and a vendor who cannot answer it is telling you something.

5. AI Tool Register

Nav: AI Tool Register. Every AI tool people can reach, including the ones you never bought.

The field that does the work

Account type is the point of this register. The same product on a business licence and on someone's personal login are two different answers, so they are two rows. Record them separately or the policy will tell staff the wrong thing.

Residual risk reads "Not scored" until you score it. It does not quietly inherit the inherent score. An auditor can tell the difference between a residual risk you assessed and one the software assumed.

13. Governance Centre, reading the flags

Nav: Governance Centre. Generated from your registers, never typed.

Deliberately trigger each flag

The fastest way to trust the list is to make it fire, then clear it.

#Do thisFlag it raises, and how to clear it
1Add a use case naming a tool not in the registerRaises a flag naming the unregistered tool. Clear it by adding the tool.
2Set a tool to Restricted, then build a use case on itRaises a restricted-tool flag. Clear it by approving the tool or discontinuing the use case.
3Leave a tool at Awaiting assessmentRaises an awaiting-decision flag. Clear it by deciding.
4Set a tool's Review by to yesterdayRaises a past-review flag. Clear it by reviewing and moving the date.
5Set a tool to Approved with no Decision ownerRaises an unowned-approval flag. Clear it by naming an owner.

What you will not find here, on purpose. There is no "N users are using unapproved AI" figure. The platform cannot see your network, so it does not pretend to. Every number on this page traces to a record you entered.

16. Templates

27 fill-in governance documents. Open any one, complete it offline, keep it with your audit pack. They complement the generated policy rather than replacing it.

17. Updates

The product changelog. Worth a glance each quarter, because a content update usually means a regulation moved. The Digital Omnibus on AI, in force 27 July 2026, is the current example: it deferred the EU AI Act's high-risk regime to December 2027 and August 2028, and softened the Article 4 literacy duty to an obligation of effort.
