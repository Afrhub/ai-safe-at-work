Employee Monitoring Policy.

What the organisation monitors on its systems, why, what it does not do, and who can look. Transparency here is a legal requirement (UK GDPR Art 13, ICO monitoring guidance 2023) and the difference between security and surveillance.

Designed to print clean. Replace every bracketed field. Staff acknowledge this document from their portal once it is live.
Policy statement

Employee Monitoring Policy of [Company Name]

Effective: [Date] · Version: 1.0 · Owner: [Role, e.g. Data Protection Lead / HR Director] · Review cycle: Annual, or on any change in processing.

1. Purpose

[Company Name] monitors some activity on its systems to keep information secure, to meet legal duties and to run its services. This policy says exactly what that means so that nobody is surprised.

3. What we do not do

  • No keystroke logging, screen recording, webcam capture or biometric monitoring.
  • No monitoring of personal devices beyond the company apps installed on them, and none at all of personal accounts.
  • No reading of message content as a matter of routine, and none of communications marked personal, except where section 4 applies and the law permits.
  • No covert monitoring except in a specific investigation of serious wrongdoing, authorised in writing by [Managing Director] with the Data Protection Lead, where telling the person would frustrate the investigation, and reviewed within [30 days].
  • No automated decisions about performance or discipline based on monitoring data.

4. When content is looked at

Content of email, files or messages is accessed only for: a security incident; a legal obligation, court order or regulator request; a formal investigation of misconduct; or continuity when a person is absent and a business need cannot wait. Each access is authorised by [role], limited to what is needed, and logged.

5. Lawful basis and safeguards

Monitoring relies on our legitimate interests in security, compliance and running the business, balanced against your privacy in a recorded legitimate interests assessment, and on legal obligation where a law requires it. A Data Protection Impact Assessment has been completed for [systems]. Monitoring data is kept for [90 days] for logs and [the period in the Retention Schedule] for training records.

6. Your rights

You may ask what monitoring data we hold about you, and to have it corrected or deleted where we no longer need it, through [privacy email]. You may raise concerns about monitoring with [HR contact] or the Data Protection Lead without detriment.

7. Working from home and personal devices

The same rules apply on company devices wherever they are. On personal devices used for work under [the BYOD policy], monitoring is limited to the company-managed apps and their data; we cannot and do not see anything else on the device.

8. Review

This policy is reviewed annually and whenever a monitoring tool is added or changed. Staff are told of changes and acknowledge the new version through the Attest AI portal.

How to roll this out

  4. Review annually and whenever a new tool, supplier or purpose is introduced.
